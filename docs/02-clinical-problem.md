### Define the event before evaluating a detector

The label must be defined before a model or dataset can be interpreted. In adult critical-care EEG, American Clinical Neurophysiology Society (ACNS) terminology distinguishes electrographic seizures, electroclinical seizures, brief potentially ictal rhythmic discharges, and the ictal-interictal continuum. An electrographic seizure can meet a discharge-frequency criterion above 2.5 Hz for at least 10 seconds or show definite evolution for at least 10 seconds; an electroclinical seizure may be shorter when a definite clinical correlate is present [V]. The guideline excludes neonates, whose event ontology and practice are different. Dataset labels may implement different rules, so a guideline definition must not silently replace a corpus annotation protocol. [Hirsch et al., ACNS 2021](https://doi.org/10.1097/WNP.0000000000000806).

**High confidence - label ontology and scoring ontology both need to be reported.** State the event definition, annotator expertise and blinding, adjudication, uncertain/ictal-interictal continuum (IIC) treatment, event matching tolerance, and whether the scored unit is a sample, window, event, record, patient, or alarm episode. Short windows cut from a labeled seizure are positive windows; that does not redefine the minimum duration of the clinical event.

### Why monitoring is difficult

Clinical scalp electroencephalography (EEG) is a high-dimensional, artifact-prone signal acquired over hours or days. Seizures can be brief, subtle, spatially focal, or obscured by movement, muscle, electrode problems, medication effects, or ICU artifacts. A detector must identify clinically meaningful events while monitoring extensive nonseizure time. For a low-prevalence event, even a respectable per-window classifier may produce many false candidates over a long record. A raw count of candidate windows also overstates notifications if overlapping positives are merged and understates burden if alerts repeat, persist, or require extensive contextual review.

Human interpretation is imperfect and time-consuming. In an adult ICU reader study, mean seizure agreement among eight experts was κ=.58 [V]; this selected reading set demonstrates reference uncertainty, not population-wide agreement. [Halford et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2014.11.008). In a bedside neonatal cohort, staff recognized 48 of 526 EEG-confirmed seizures (9%) [V]; those data motivate neonatal monitoring assistance but do not estimate performance in adults. [Murray et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1136/adc.2005.086314).

Acquisition delay is a different problem from detection. Rapid EEG workflow studies examine time to recording and access; they should not be presented as AI detector efficacy. Likewise, continuous-versus-routine EEG trials address monitoring strategy, not model accuracy. More detected events or treatment changes do not automatically establish improved outcomes.

### What clinical and workflow studies show

| Evidence | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Prospective epilepsy monitoring unit (EMU) detector performance.** Furbass et al. assessed EpiScan in 205 people across three EMUs; reported sensitivity for unequivocal electrographic seizures was 81%, with 7.1 false alarms/day [V]. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2014.09.023). | Prospective multicentre performance in a defined EMU workflow. | A randomized staffing, safety, cost, or outcome benefit; broad ICU/ambulatory generalization. |
| **Retrospective EMU response opportunity.** Rommens et al. analyzed 188 seizures in 115 people and a separate false-alarm sample of 617 hours from 30 people. Staff/Encevis/BESA detected 67%/67%/65%; median latencies were 31/10/14 s, and false alarms were 4.9/2.1 per 24 h for Encevis/BESA [V]. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yebeh.2018.04.026). | Algorithms may signal events earlier than staff recognition in retrospective data. | That real-time alerts changed staff response: algorithms were applied retrospectively, not delivered as a live randomized intervention. |
| **Randomized neonatal assistance (ANSeR).** Seizure-hour recognition 66.0% vs 45.3%; individual-neonate sensitivity 81.3% vs 89.5%, not significantly better with assistance; inappropriate ASM use did not differ significantly [V]. [Pavel et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2352-4642(20)30239-X). | A bounded process benefit in neonatal care. | Improved individual-level sensitivity, medication appropriateness, or long-term outcome. |
| **Adult inpatient continuous EEG (cEEG) system evaluation.** Ganguly et al. report an expanded retrospective cohort of 7,924 records from 2,854 people. Record-level sensitivity was 0.91 and record-level positive predictive value (PPV) 0.08 [V]; PPV means positive-record precision, not event-alarm precision. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000200267). | Performance at identifying records containing seizures in one health system. | Event timing or event precision for the expanded cohort; the report lacked precise seizure timing for those records. |
| **EEG review aid (compressed spectral array (CSA)).** Moura et al. compared compressed spectral array guided review with conventional review over 118 adult cEEGs and 2,092 h. Review time was 8±4 vs 38±17 min per 24 h, with sensitivity 87.3% [V]. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000000537). | A workflow visualization that can shorten expert review in the studied service. | A standalone automated detector or evidence that every missed event is recovered; raw EEG review remained part of workflow. |
| **External inference plus small review pilot.** Yang et al. report TUH-trained inference on 1,006 Australian sessions/~14,590 h, 76.68% sensitivity and ~56 false alarms/day; a separate 66-session pilot reported 92.19% recognition and review time 7.62 vs 90 min [V] in the primary abstract. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2022.118083). | A material external-inference result and a separate human-assisted pilot. | A randomized live-deployment effect, general workflow savings across services, or patient outcome benefit. The primary abstract does not provide the full methods or confidence intervals. |
| **Continuous monitoring strategy (Continuous EEG Randomized Trial in Adults (CERTA)).** Randomized comparison showed more seizures detected and more treatment changes with continuous rather than routine EEG, without demonstrated mortality benefit [V]. [Rossetti et al.](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2020.2264). | Causal evidence about monitoring strategy in the enrolled adult cohort. | AI detector utility. |

**Moderate confidence - process benefits are real but bounded.** Do not collapse event-level detection, record-level sensitivity, seizure-hour recognition, earlier retrospective flagging, reduced data selection, measured review minutes, or patient outcomes into a single "clinical benefit" claim. Roy et al. report raw-data selection reduction for annotation assistance, but measured clinician-time savings and outcomes were not established in the cited studies. [Roy et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2021.103275).

### Measuring workload and clinical value

A useful evaluation records electrographic onset/offset, time the input becomes available, algorithm flag, confirmation, notification, and staff action separately. Report false alarm episodes per monitored day (with exposure denominator), repeated notifications, total alarm duration, fraction of EEG selected for review, review minutes, seizure burden captured, onset delay, offset error, and patient-level distributions. Specify whether false alarms are divided by total recording or nonseizure time. Report pooled results alongside patient-macro distributions, seizure-free people, and worst-performing site/device strata.

A detector may support triage, review prioritization, or continuous surveillance without replacing expert interpretation. The intended user and action determine an acceptable operating point. That point cannot be selected post hoc on the test site and then described as frozen external validation.

The intended use should be named: offline screening of long records, prioritizing candidate segments for review, real-time bedside notification, ambulatory event capture, neonatal trend support, or patient-specific closed-loop stimulation. These use cases have different latency, sensitivity, alarm burden, sensor, and escalation requirements. A closed-loop implant is not interchangeable with an EEG review aid. Monetary cost of false alarms was **not found** as a directly measured endpoint in the cited studies; report alarm episodes and reviewer time before assigning a dollar burden.
