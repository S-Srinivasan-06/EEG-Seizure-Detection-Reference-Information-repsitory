# 11. Research gaps and technology triage

[Contents](../README.md) | [Previous: Part 10](10-state-of-the-art.md) | [Next: Part 12](12-open-questions.md)

Clinical importance means the likely consequence of resolving the gap for the stated use, not evidence that a solution will improve outcomes. Evidence strength describes evidence that the gap exists and/or the current limits; tractability estimates whether a credible study can address it. Scores are categorical (High/Medium/Low).

**Prominent prospective-evidence gap:** Prospective workflow effect and patient benefit are separate, high-importance gaps, even though a workflow trial should follow external and silent-mode safety gates. Bounded evidence exists: Yang reports a 66-session assisted-review pilot [V] separately from frozen external inference, and ANSeR is a randomized neonatal assistance trial; broad adult workload and patient-outcome benefit remain unestablished. [Yang](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2022.118083), [Pavel](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2352-4642(20)30239-X)

| Rank | Gap | Clinical importance | Evidence strength | Tractability | Why it matters / what would close it |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **External validation and domain shift** across hospitals, devices, acquisition protocols, and care settings | High | Medium | Medium | Patient-held-out testing is not site/device validation. Peh reports cross-dataset event testing, and Yang reports frozen TUH-to-RPAH inference, but neither establishes prospective broad clinical effectiveness. Require frozen-model cohort/site evaluation with no target-label tuning; separate retrospective external performance from live workflow benefit. [Peh et al.](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129065723500120), [Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2022.118083) |
| 2 | **Patient-independent generalization and leakage control** | High | High | High | Siena segment holdout scored 79.1% accuracy [V] versus 65.1% [V] with subject holdout in one study; this is evidence about that experiment, not a universal correction factor. Lock patient-disjoint train/validation/test partitions and audit all derived windows, pretraining exposure, normalization, and repeated records. [Brookshire et al.](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2024.1373515) |
| 3 | **Rare seizure types, short events, and patient/site subgroups** | High | Low | Medium | Missing rare morphologies or brief seizures may be clinically consequential even when aggregate sensitivity is acceptable. Publish event counts and patient-level performance by prespecified seizure type, duration, state, and site; use uncertainty intervals and avoid unsupported subgroup conclusions. |
| 4 | **Montage, missing-channel, electrode-placement, and hardware robustness** | High | Medium | Medium | Channel masking or selected scalp derivations do not prove performance on a new montage or true behind-ear device. Test actual signal sources, references, channel reorderings, electrode loss, and cross-device transfer. SeizeIT1/2 are inpatient video-EEG datasets despite wearable sensors. [SeizeIT1 record](https://doi.org/10.48804/P5Q0OJ), [SeizeIT2 dataset](https://doi.org/10.18112/openneuro.ds005873.v1.1.0), [Hartmann et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.17259) |
| 5 | **False alarms under natural prevalence and long negative exposure** | High | High | High | Seizure-enriched/pruned clip sets do not estimate real-world precision or workload. Report event sensitivity together with false alarms per 24 hours (including non-seizure hours), event precision, and patient distributions at a user-agreed operating point. Yang's external result combined 76.68% sensitivity [V] with nearly 56 false alarms per 24 hours [V]; the separate small reviewer pilot is not the same evaluation. [TUSZ corpus](https://doi.org/10.3389/fninf.2018.00083), [Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2022.118083) |
| 6 | **Alarm calibration, threshold choice, and operating-point stability** | High | Medium | Medium | A score is not a probability unless calibration is established, and thresholds may shift by cohort or prevalence. Prespecify threshold selection on training/validation data, assess reliability and Brier score on untouched patients/sites, and report calibration drift without retuning on test data. |
| 7 | **Artifact, physiologic mimic, and signal-quality robustness** | High | Medium | Medium | Movement, muscle, electrode pops, ECG contamination, and rhythmic non-ictal activity can trigger flags or obscure seizures. Add clinically reviewed artifact strata, signal-quality failure detection, and blinded challenge sets; report false alarms and missed events under each condition. Historical systems explicitly treated artifact handling as central. [Gotman](https://doi.org/10.1016/0013-4694(82)90038-4) |
| 8 | **Reference-label uncertainty and event ontology** | High | High | Medium | Define electrographic/electroclinical seizure, BIRDs, and ictal-interictal-continuum treatment per setting; preserve indeterminate labels and report adjudication. Eight experts showed mean seizure κ=.58 [V] on selected ICU excerpts; labels are a reference, not biological ground truth. [ACNS terminology](https://doi.org/10.1097/WNP.0000000000000806), [Halford et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2014.11.008) |
| 9 | **Latency and causal real-time operation** | High | Medium | High | Offline window inference can use future context or conceal acquisition, buffering, and alert delays. Measure onset-to-alert end-to-end in causal chronological streaming, separately from model-only time; publish missed-event/delay distributions. |
| 10 | **Reproducibility, protocol completeness, and standards conformance** | High | High | High | Exact patients, partitions, labels, preprocessing, score aggregation, thresholds, uncertainty, and event matching determine what a result means. The DGDCN paper has table/prose discrepancies and an available but not reproduced repository with apparent issues; this is not proof of test tuning. Use versioned data/configs and SzCORE conventions. [DGDCN paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering12080832), [code](https://github.com/Open-EXG/DGDCN-EEG-Seizure-Detection), [SzCORE framework](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18113) |
| 11 | **Interpretability and explanation validity** | High | High | Medium | Explanations may aid review, but graph weights or saliency are not physiological connectivity or seizure causality. Test explanations against expert localization/perturbations and usability outcomes; report predictive associations only. [Nolte et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2004.04.029) |
| 12 | **Age and setting transportability** | High | High | Medium | Neonatal algorithms and adult scalp/ICU detectors cannot be pooled as a single population. ANSeR improved seizure-hour recognition but did not demonstrate improved individual-neonate sensitivity or long-term outcome. Develop separate age/setting protocols and validate separately. [Pavel et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2352-4642(20)30239-X) |
| 13 | **Fairness and unequal performance** by age, sex, race/ethnicity where available, language/site, disability, and access context | High | Low | Medium | Missing subgroup reporting makes it impossible to tell who bears missed seizures or excess alarms. Prespecify ethically and legally appropriate subgroup analyses, report data availability and uncertainty, and investigate differences before deployment; do not infer fairness from a pooled score. |
| 14 | **Prospective workflow effect, clinician workload, and patient outcomes** | High | Medium | Medium | Evidence is not absent: Yang's 66-session [V] pilot reports reduced review time; the ANSeR randomized neonatal trial improved a seizure-hour endpoint; Rommens is retrospective, and Yang's large external inference test is separate from its small pilot. No broad adult workload or patient-outcome benefit follows from these findings. Measure time-motion, recognition, actions, harms, and costs prospectively. [Yang](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2022.118083), [Pavel](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2352-4642(20)30239-X), [Rommens](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yebeh.2018.04.026) |
| 15 | **Privacy, governance, and data access for external validation** | High | Low | Medium | Multicenter data linkage, detailed time series, and labels require lawful access, security, and governance. Report deidentification, access controls, retention, permitted reuse, and whether split manifests/code can be shared; use federated or controlled-access evaluation only if it preserves auditable patient/site separation. |
| 16 | **Compute, energy, uptime, and deployment reliability** | High | Medium | High | A compact model may still be slow or brittle after filtering, buffering, and UI integration. Report named hardware, model size, memory, real-time factor, per-window latency distribution, energy where measured, dropout/recovery, and end-to-end uptime. |
| 17 | **Standards, intended use, and regulatory pathway** | High | Medium | Medium | SzCORE helps standardize research data/scoring but is not product clearance. Define intended users, decisions, population, risk controls, software lifecycle, clinical evaluation, and applicable jurisdictional requirements before moving from research to clinical support. Do not describe research metrics as regulatory validation. [SzCORE](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18113) |

### Technology triage: mechanisms and decisive tests

| Candidate | Target gap | Why it could help | Existing seizure-specific evidence | Decisive next experiment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temporal CNN/RNN and signal-feature baselines | Missed event evolution and weak reproducibility comparisons | Temporal filters capture evolving morphology; simple baselines expose whether added architecture contributes beyond time context | Patient-specific and supervised temporal methods exist, but reported cohorts, splits, and scoring differ. [Shoeb & Guttag](https://physionet.org/physiobank/database/chbmit/shoeb-icml-2010.pdf), [Saab et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-020-0264-0) | Lock patient-disjoint continuous folds; compare matched receptive fields and tuning budget against the proposed model using event sensitivity, false alarms/24 h, delay, calibration and external cohort results. |
| Dilated temporal convolution / DGDCN | Long temporal context without prohibitively deep stacks | Dilations expand receptive field at modest depth and may represent onset evolution | DGDCN reports TUSZ clip results, not continuous alarm or external-site performance; publication and repository concerns remain unreproduced. [DGDCN](https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering12080832), [repository](https://github.com/Open-EXG/DGDCN-EEG-Seizure-Detection) | Reproduce first; ablate dilations against a parameter/FLOP-matched ordinary temporal CNN on locked patient-held-out event data. Keep only if an external event-level gain exceeds uncertainty without worsening alarms or latency. |
| Fixed or learned graph/channel interaction | Cross-channel propagation and montage relationships | Joint channel information could distinguish evolving seizure patterns from local artifact | Dynamic graph seizure models exist mainly with clip/task and split limitations; scalp sensor graphs do not establish physiological connectivity. [DGDCN](https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering12080832), [Nolte et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2004.04.029) | On identical data and compute budget, compare graph-free mixer, fixed sensor topology, static learned graph, and dynamic adjacency; test missing channels and external montage transfer. Use patient-clustered uncertainty and no causal interpretation of weights. |
| CNN/RNN versus transformer encoder | Patient/site generalization and longer context | Attention can integrate distant windows and variable channel context | EEG foundation and transformer work includes seizure clip tasks, but also abnormality/event classification; this evidence does not establish continuous clinical alarms. [CBraMod](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/bbbd6d915cb90be21c1254a82d45cedd-Paper-Conference.pdf), [BENDR](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2021.653659) | Match input, training data, parameter/FLOP budget and tuning effort; audit pretraining exposure, then compare event utility, calibration and external site transfer,not clip AUC alone. |
| Seizure-specific self-supervised pretraining (PPi) | Limited expert-labeled seizure examples in iEEG | Channel/context pretraining may improve representation with fewer event labels | PPi reports patient-separated SEEG experiments, but small cohorts and segment/channel endpoints do not establish continuous external alarms or scalp transfer. [PPi](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-3047) | Within iEEG only, compare pretrained and scratch models at identical patient splits and labeled-data budgets; test a locked external cohort with event metrics and alarms/time. |
| Weak supervision from clinical workflow labels | Expensive and sparse expert event labels | Noisy routine labels can scale training data and reduce exhaustive manual labeling | Saab supports weak-label model training; labels are imperfect, while quoted labor savings are counterfactual rather than time-motion measurements. [Saab et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-020-0264-0) | Train with weak labels, then evaluate against blinded expert-gold continuous data; quantify label error, event utility, adjudication burden and actual reader minutes. |
| Reduced-channel / behind-ear inputs | Access, comfort, and wearable acquisition constraints | Fewer electrodes could make prolonged monitoring easier, if spatial coverage retains target events | Selected two-channel scalp triage and inpatient behind-ear datasets exist; actual outpatient performance and adherence remain open. [Hartmann](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.17259), [SeizeIT2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-05580-x) | Prospectively test the actual intended sensors against concurrent full scalp reference, including missed-event coverage, alarms/day, usable hours, adherence and external patients; do not substitute scalp-derived channels for wearable validation. |
| Calibration, causal streaming, quality gates and alarm logic | Alarm burden, drift, artifacts, and end-to-end delay | Converts a score into a stable, actionable stream and can expose unusable signal rather than silently misclassify it | These are evaluation/system requirements, not benefits proved by clip classifiers; SzCORE standardizes event/sample metrics. [SzCORE](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18113) | Freeze threshold/calibrator and run long chronological silent-mode tests; measure false alarms/24 h, missed events, end-to-end latency, reliability, uptime, signal-quality failures and site drift before any workflow trial. |
