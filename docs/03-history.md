# How EEG seizure-detection methods developed

Evidence date: 30 September 2026. This is a bounded search, not an exhaustive systematic review. See the [evidence policy](evidence-policy.md).

[Previous chapter](02-clinical-problem.md) | [Chapter index](../README.md) | [Next chapter](04-representations.md)


This roadmap describes families of methods and the evidential shift between them. It is not a leaderboard: patient populations, modalities, sampling, annotation, splits, and metric units differ. A newer architecture is not automatically a stronger clinical detector.

| Era / family | Core representation and contribution | Examples and evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Rule-based temporal detection (1980s onward)** | Hand-designed rhythmicity, amplitude, frequency, morphology, and persistence rules summarize signal patterns and reduce review burden. | Gotman (1982) describes rhythmic elementary-wave rules and artifact-related false detections; the work frames computer detection as data reduction. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0013-4694(82)90038-4). Rules remain useful transparent baselines and postprocessors. |
| **Signal processing and engineered features (1990s-2010s)** | Band power, energy, entropy, wavelets, spectral/time-frequency summaries, and handcrafted morphology turn a waveform into a feature vector. | Tzallas et al. (2009) combine time-frequency fractional energy and an ANN; Guo et al. (2010) use multiwavelet entropy and ANN. [Tzallas DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TITB.2009.2017939); [Guo DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneumeth.2010.08.030). Selected segment benchmarks do not alone prove continuous alarm utility. |
| **Classical supervised and patient-specific models (2000s-2010s)** | SVMs and related classifiers learn decision boundaries from selected temporal/spectral features; personalization can exploit an individual's known seizure morphology. | Shoeb and Guttag (2010) describe patient-specific support vector machine (SVM) detection in CHB-MIT. [ICML paper](https://physionet.org/physiobank/database/chbmit/shoeb-icml-2010.pdf). Personalization differs from zero-shot cross-patient transfer and requires reporting adaptation burden. |
| **Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and temporal convolution (2010s)** | Convolutions learn local waveform or time-frequency motifs; deeper or dilated temporal convolutional networks (TCNs) expand receptive field while parallelizing sequence computation. | CNNs were motivated by hand-feature limits: representation learning can combine morphology and spatial channels. EEGNet shows compact temporal/depthwise spatial filtering, but its original evaluation is BCI, not seizure detection. [Lawhern et al.](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2552/aace8c). Acharya et al. (2018 online 2017) study selected seizure states; EEGWaveNet uses multiscale spatiotemporal convolution. [Acharya](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compbiomed.2017.09.017); [Thuwajit](https://doi.org/10.1109/TII.2021.3133307). These demonstrate learned features/clip methods, not that CNNs universally capture clinically relevant spatial propagation or continuous alarms. |
| **Recurrent neural networks (RNNs), long short-term memory (LSTM), gated recurrent units (GRUs), and CNN-RNN hybrids (2010s)** | Recurrent state updates model ordered temporal context; CNN-RNN hybrids first extract local patterns then summarize their sequence. | Motivated by fixed-window models' limited history and evolving seizure morphology; Golmohammadi et al. (2019) is a deep CNN/recurrent example. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2019.00076). Long context is an architectural capacity, not empirical proof of better onset/offset or alarm rates. Report causal direction, context length, and patient-separated event results. |
| **Attention and graph models (late 2010s-2020s)** | Attention weights channels or times; graph models encode electrodes/contacts as nodes and relations as edges. Variants include fixed distance graphs, sample-specific correlation graphs, coherence/phase coupling, learnable adjacency, graph attention (GAT), spatiotemporal graphs, and graph transformers. | Tang et al. (ICLR 2022) use distance and sample-specific correlation graphs with self-supervised learning. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=k9bx1EfHI_-). DGDCN's implementation modulates fixed Chebyshev supports with sample-specific attention; its selected temporal attention is noncausal. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering12080832); [code](https://github.com/Open-EXG/DGDCN-EEG-Seizure-Detection). These variants encode engineering assumptions; a distance edge is not a functional connection and correlation/coherence can reflect volume conduction. Imaginary coherency was proposed to reduce zero-lag volume-conduction effects, but no learned graph attention proves anatomical or causal connectivity. [Nolte et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2004.04.029). |
| **Domain adaptation and transfer (2010s-2020s)** | Distribution-alignment losses, adversarial domains, fine-tuning, or calibration seek to reduce patient/site shift. | Wang et al. (2024) compare shallow MK-MMD and deep adversarial alignment across CHB-MIT/Siena. [Primary abstract](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129065724500552). Such work motivates explicit target-exposure audits: unlabeled target adaptation, supervised personalization, and untouched external tests answer different questions. |
| **Transformers and channel-flexible models (2020s)** | Self-attention supports long context, flexible channel tokens, and spatial-temporal interaction; convolutional stems often retain local EEG inductive bias. | BIOT reports explicit CHB-MIT clip detection with variable-channel tokenization, not event-alarm proof. [NeurIPS paper](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-3420). LaBraM uses masked quantized spectral tokens; its verified tasks are TUAB abnormality and TUEV event-type classification, not seizure-event detection. [ICLR paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/47393e8594c82ce8fd83adc672cf9872-Paper-Conference.pdf). CBraMod includes a CHB-MIT 10-s binary clip task; cases 1-19 train and 20-21 validate, but case 21 repeats the person in case 1, so train/validation are not fully person-independent. Its test set remains a separate issue. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07236); [CHB-MIT release](https://physionet.org/content/chbmit/1.0.0/). |
| **Self-supervised, weakly supervised, and seizure-specific pretraining** | Self-supervised learning (SSL) uses unlabeled context or masked prediction; weak supervision uses noisy report/rule labels; task-specific pretraining targets seizure-relevant structure. These reduce dependence on dense expert labels but do not guarantee label validity or transfer. | BENDR is a general EEG contrastive-pretraining lineage, not clinical seizure proof. [Kostas et al.](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2021.653659). Saab et al. demonstrate weak supervision from labeling functions and reports on clinical EEG; noisy functions can transmit systematic errors. [Saab DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-020-0264-0). PPi is seizure-specific pretraining for intracranial stereo-electroencephalography (SEEG), with patient-group separation on two public cohorts and a small private 5/1/1 patient split; the evaluation report does not specify continuous alarms per hour. [NeurIPS paper](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-3047). |
| **Foundation-scale EEG models (2023-2026)** | Large pretraining corpora and shared encoders aim to support multiple tasks; claims need to be traced to the named downstream dataset, unit, split, and protocol. | LaBraM's verified tasks are listed above. REVE reports large pretraining scale, but its enumerated downstream task suite did not identify a seizure-specific protocol despite abstract language; the paper protocol does not specify that endpoint. [REVE paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/20a917f77773ac0fa8bea2bdd6606b66-Paper-Conference.pdf). The SzCORE challenge does not establish a general foundation-model benefit. |
| **Multimodal and wearable systems** | Behind-ear/subscalp EEG can be paired with ECG, motion, EMG, or diaries; candidate generation and expert review form a pipeline. Reduced spatial coverage changes the detection task. | SeizeIT2 validation reports algorithm sensitivity 0.73 and precision 0.004 before review, and sensitivity 0.31/precision 0.83 after review [V]; post-hoc sensitivity 0.74 applies only to seizures with clear behind-ear EEG pattern and ictal tachycardia. This was inpatient vEEG, not home validation. [Swinnen et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/epi4.70260). UMPIRE is prospective subscalp acquisition/feasibility evidence, not exhaustive detector validation. [Halliday et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18458). |

**Interpretation.** The field has progressed from hand-specified signal rules to learned features and high-capacity sequence models, while the most consequential barriers remain stable: label ambiguity, scarce patient-independent positive examples, large negative exposure, cohort/site shift, and deployment constraints. Graphs and attention offer representational choices, not evidence that learned dependencies are physiological. Foundation pretraining may improve sample efficiency; it cannot fix an incorrect target label, identity overlap, or absent continuous external testing.
