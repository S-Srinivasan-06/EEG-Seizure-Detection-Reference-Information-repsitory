# Executive synthesis: what seizure-detection evidence supports

Evidence date: 30 September 2026. This is a bounded search, not an exhaustive systematic review. See the [evidence policy](evidence-policy.md).

[Chapter index](../README.md) | [Next chapter](02-clinical-problem.md)


Seizure detection using electroencephalography (EEG) is a family of related but distinct tasks. A model that labels a short EEG window as seizure-like has not thereby demonstrated reliable onset-to-offset detection in continuous monitoring, acceptable alarm burden, transfer to a new hospital, or clinical benefit. Those claims require separate evaluation. The relevant evidence chain is acquisition algorithm candidate expert confirmation staff action appropriate treatment patient outcome. An improvement at one step cannot stand in for evidence at the next.

**High confidence - benchmark discrimination and clinical alarm utility are different endpoints.** Window accuracy/area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC)/area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC), event sensitivity, record sensitivity, false alarms per hour/day, alarm latency, and review time have different units and answer different questions. Continuous negatives and patient-level separation are essential when claiming alarm performance or generalization. The current SzCORE challenge illustrates the distinction: revised, non-peer-reviewed v2 reports a best event F1 of 32% (sensitivity 37%, precision 29%) on a private, held-out continuous scalp EEG set of 65 people and 4,360 hours [V]; these are challenge-specific results, not a universal ceiling. [SzCORE challenge v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18191v2).

**Moderate confidence - useful detection and assistance have been demonstrated in bounded clinical settings.** Prospective multicentre epilepsy monitoring unit (EMU) software assessment exists; adult retrospective external inference and small review pilots exist; and neonatal randomized assistance improved seizure-hour recognition. These results do not establish a universal detector or broad adult patient-outcome benefit. In ANSeR, seizure-hour recognition was 66.0% with the algorithm versus 45.3% with conventional care [V], while individual-neonate sensitivity did not improve significantly and inappropriate antiseizure medication use did not differ significantly. [Pavel et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2352-4642(20)30239-X).

**Moderate confidence - scalp signal visibility limits what scalp benchmarks can claim about intracranial seizures.** In a simultaneous scalp/stereo-electroencephalography (SEEG) cohort, scalp ictal patterns were identified in 100 of 172 SEEG seizures; visibility was 33% for focal-aware and 33% for subclinical events [V]. This is a selected surgical cohort and human signal-visibility study, not an algorithmic sensitivity ceiling. [Casale et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1097/WNP.0000000000000739).

**Emerging - pretraining and weak supervision address label scarcity, but transportable alarms remain unestablished.** General EEG representation models, seizure-specific intracranial pretraining, self-supervised graph methods, and report-derived weak labels should be compared on their actual downstream tasks. TUEV event-type classification and TUAB normal/abnormal classification are not seizure-event detection. PPi is an important seizure-specific SEEG comparator, but its reported P/R/F scores do not establish continuous event false-alarm performance. [PPi proceedings paper](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-3047); [Saab et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-020-0264-0).

**Benchmark-method adoption evidence is bounded.** A 2025 peer-reviewed EMBC review selected 19 eligible papers and reimplemented three; its abstract reports discrepancies after standardizing performance, but the broader sensitivity/precision summaries are internally unlike its concluding approximate figures and should not be pooled. A global state-of-the-art (SOTA) adoption count was **not found**. [Samanos, Dan & Atienza 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254038).

The strongest next study is a locked, patient- and site-separated external evaluation of the full detection-and-review pipeline on continuously annotated data, followed by a randomized reader/workflow study. Prespecify target event ontology, annotation uncertainty, threshold, postprocessing, alarm suppression, compute and latency, and the clinical action. Keep scalp EMU, adult ICU, neonatal ICU, intracranial EEG, wearable, and multimodal cohorts as distinct strata with separate references and outcomes.
