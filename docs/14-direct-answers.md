# 14. Direct answers to 20 questions

[Contents](../README.md) | [Previous: Part 13](13-research-program.md) | [Next: Part 15](15-evidence-and-references.md)

### Key terms

- **Clip classification:** assigning a label to a short EEG segment. This differs from continuous event detection, which must identify event timing in a long stream.
- **Event-level metric:** a measure based on seizure events, such as event sensitivity or onset delay. Sample-level metrics score individual time points or windows.
- **GNN / graph model:** a neural network that represents channels or sensors as nodes and their relationships as edges. Learned edges are predictive model components and do not by themselves establish physiological connectivity.
- **AUPRC / AUROC:** area under the precision-recall curve and receiver operating characteristic curve; both summarize performance across thresholds, but neither alone gives false alarms per 24 hours at a chosen operating point.
- **Confidence grade:** a judgment about the support and scope of an answer, not a detector performance score.
- **CNN / RNN / GNN:** convolutional / recurrent / graph neural network. **iEEG / SEEG** means intracranial EEG / stereoelectroencephalography. **TUSZ** means Temple University Hospital Seizure Corpus. **EMU / ICU** means epilepsy monitoring unit / intensive care unit.

The answers summarize the conclusions developed in the preceding chapters. Each confidence judgment applies to the stated conclusion and includes its reason.

1. **What is the actual unresolved problem?** The central unresolved problem is whether a locked seizure detector can deliver useful continuous event recognition for new patients at new hospitals and devices, at a clinically acceptable alarm burden, within a defined workflow. Clip classification and within-cohort retrospective scores do not answer that combined question. **Confidence: high, because current standards and external studies distinguish these endpoints.** [SzCORE](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18113)

2. **What evidence shows that this problem matters?** In one Siena study, segment-held-out accuracy was 79.1% [V] versus 65.1% [V] with subject holdout; a large external TUH-to-RPAH test reported 76.68% sensitivity [V] and nearly 56 false alarms per 24 hours [V]. These are different studies and metrics, but together show why patient separation and alarm burden both matter. **Confidence: high for those study-specific findings, not for a universal effect size.** [Brookshire et al.](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2024.1373515), [Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2022.118083)

3. **What has been established?** No universal, cross-setting solution is established, although bounded problems have been addressed: external event testing exists, prospective EMU performance has been studied, and neonatal decision support improved a seizure-hour recognition endpoint. None alone establishes broad adult workflow or patient-outcome benefit. **Confidence: moderate-high, because evidence exists but covers distinct populations and endpoints.** [Peh et al.](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129065723500120), [Fürbass et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2014.09.023), [Pavel et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2352-4642(20)30239-X)

4. **Is progress easy because data are plentiful or artifacts are weak?** No: selected, seizure-enriched or pruned datasets can support algorithm development, but they may not preserve natural prevalence, long negative exposure, representative artifacts, or external site variation. The TUSZ corpus is valuable yet should not by itself be treated as a natural-prevalence continuous alarm test. **Confidence: high for the distinction, while artifact coverage varies by dataset.** [TUSZ](https://doi.org/10.3389/fninf.2018.00083), [SzCORE](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18113)

5. **Has signal processing solved seizure detection?** No signal representation removes the need to define events, labels, splits, thresholds, and alarm costs. Spectral, waveform, entropy, and time-frequency methods provide useful features or baselines, but their segment results do not establish generalizable continuous event utility. **Confidence: high, because this is an endpoint distinction supported across the benchmark literature.** [Tzallas et al.](https://doi.org/10.1109/TITB.2009.2017939), [Guo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneumeth.2010.08.030), [SzCORE](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18113)

6. **Have CNNs and RNNs solved it, or failed?** Temporal CNN/RNN methods have demonstrated utility in defined tasks, including some patient-independent and external evaluations. Their performance remains dependent on the population, acquisition, and evaluation protocol. They are strong baselines for event-level external comparisons, not a settled universal solution. **Confidence: moderate-high, because the evidence supports both utility and remaining transport limits.** [Saab et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-020-0264-0), [Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2022.118083)

7. **What has a GNN added?** A graph model can represent cross-channel relations explicitly, creating a testable inductive bias for spatial context. Existing seizure graph studies do not yet show that graph complexity independently improves external event utility, and learned edges do not prove physiological connectivity. **Confidence: moderate, because the mechanism is plausible but decisive comparison evidence is limited.** [DGDCN](https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering12080832), [Nolte et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2004.04.029)

8. **Do dynamic graphs improve on static graphs?** The inspected evidence does not establish that dynamic adjacency is better than fixed topology, static learned graphs, or a graph-free channel mixer under matched evaluation. A matched ablation on patient-disjoint continuous data with an external test is needed. **Confidence: moderate, because the comparative evidence is insufficient rather than clearly negative.** [DGDCN](https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering12080832), [Nafea et al.](https://doi.org/10.3390/ai6060120)

9. **Is DGDCN convincing?** It is a plausible clip-classification hypothesis, but its published TUSZ scores do not establish continuous alarms or external validity, and table/prose discrepancies plus unreproduced code concerns limit confidence. Those issues justify careful reproduction; they do not prove test-set tuning or invalidate the architecture. **Confidence: high for this scoped assessment.** [DGDCN paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering12080832), [public repository](https://github.com/Open-EXG/DGDCN-EEG-Seizure-Detection)

10. **Have transformers, self-supervision, and foundation models solved it?** No: some papers evaluate seizure clips or seizure-specific intracranial tasks, while others evaluate abnormality or event-type classification; these endpoints cannot be merged into continuous seizure-detection evidence. Pretraining is promising, but seizure-event benefit must be tested with exposure audits and external splits. **Confidence: high for endpoint distinctions, moderate for future promise.** [CBraMod](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/bbbd6d915cb90be21c1254a82d45cedd-Paper-Conference.pdf), [LaBraM official paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/47393e8594c82ce8fd83adc672cf9872-Paper-Conference.pdf), [PPi](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-3047)

11. **Do models generalize across patients and hospitals?** Patient-disjoint evaluations exist, and cross-dataset/event or frozen external evaluations exist, so neither dimension is wholly unstudied. Performance is still cohort-specific, and patient holdout alone does not establish hospital/device transfer. **Confidence: high for the distinction; moderate for breadth of generalization.** [Peh et al.](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129065723500120), [Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2022.118083), [SzCORE](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18113)

12. **How large is the generalization gap?** There is no single field-wide number because reported studies differ in task, population, prevalence, split, reference, and scoring unit. One Siena experiment's 79.1% versus 65.1% [V] accuracy comparison and the revised challenge's 32% event F1 [V] are informative examples, not interchangeable estimates. **Confidence: high that one universal magnitude is unsupported.** [Brookshire et al.](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2024.1373515), [SzCORE challenge v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18191v2)

13. **How good is human agreement on seizure labels?** It is imperfect in studied ICU excerpts: eight experts had mean seizure κ=.58 [V], so a single reader label should not be treated as biological truth. Agreement and adjudication may differ across settings, and uncertainty should be represented explicitly. **Confidence: moderate-high for the cited ICU sample, not every EEG setting.** [Halford et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2014.11.008), [ACNS terminology](https://doi.org/10.1097/WNP.0000000000000806)

14. **Why do studies appear to conflict?** Often they answer different questions: segment versus event scoring, patient versus site holdout, scalp-visible versus intracranial events, and model performance versus clinical workflow. Where studies use comparable endpoints, differences can still reflect population, prevalence, annotation, or implementation; the evidence should be reconciled at that level before claiming a contradiction. **Confidence: high as a methodological explanation; each empirical discrepancy needs its own audit.** [SzCORE](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18113), [Casale et al.](https://doi.org/10.1097/WNP.0000000000000739)

15. **Which claims are overstated?** Common overstatements equate clip AUC with continuous detection, patient holdout with external validity, AI flags with faster staff action, or attention maps with physiology. Workload claims also need precise scope: Yang's external test and separate 66-session review pilot [V] are distinct, and its reported time reduction does not establish broad routine-service savings. **Confidence: high, based on corrected source-level distinctions.** [Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2022.118083), [Rommens et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yebeh.2018.04.026), [Nolte et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2004.04.029)

16. **What are the strongest facts?** Continuous event metrics, false alarms over negative exposure, and patient/site-held-out testing answer questions that clip scores cannot; external testing and bounded prospective assistance do exist. In the revised challenge report, best event F1 was 32%, sensitivity 37%, and precision 29% [V], but it is a non-peer-reviewed, versioned result rather than a universal benchmark. **Confidence: high for the cited evidence and its limits.** [SzCORE v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18191v2), [Peh et al.](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129065723500120), [Pavel et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2352-4642(20)30239-X)

17. **What remains unknown?** The intended operating point, rare-event sensitivity, calibration drift, subgroup disparities, real device/montage robustness, and broad workflow or patient-outcome effects remain unresolved. Evidence is especially thin for prospective adult workload benefit; existing bounded evidence should not be called absent. **Confidence: moderate-high, since the dossier audit found gaps but was not an exhaustive systematic review.** [Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2022.118083), [SzCORE](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18113)

18. **Which technologies could close the gap?** Better external data and validation, reliable labels, calibrated causal streaming, artifact/channel robustness, and usable alarm workflows are more directly tied to the gap than architectural novelty alone. Temporal CNNs, graph branches, transformers, or pretraining should be retained only if matched event-level tests show added external utility. **Confidence: moderate-high as a research priority, not a proven winner.** [SzCORE](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18113), [DGDCN](https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering12080832)

19. **What experiment would provide the most information?** A preregistered, patient-disjoint continuous benchmark with a frozen external hospital/device cohort, natural negative exposure, adjudicated labels, and matched graph-free/graph/temporal baselines would directly test the biggest uncertainty. It should report event sensitivity at a user-agreed false-alarm burden, delay, calibration, compute, subgroup/site distributions, and clustered uncertainty. **Confidence: high as an information-priority judgment; exact acceptance targets remain proposed design choices.** [SzCORE](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18113), [Peh et al.](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129065723500120)

20. **What should be built next?** Build a reproducible, causal scalp-streaming research successor with a strong graph-free baseline, optional temporal and graph branches, explicit missing-channel handling, calibrated event outputs, and measured end-to-end latency. Validate it externally and remove any component without robust independent event utility; develop ICU, neonatal, wearable, and iEEG systems separately. **Confidence: moderate-high as a proposed research direction, not a claim of established superiority.** [DGDCN](https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering12080832), [SzCORE](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18113)
