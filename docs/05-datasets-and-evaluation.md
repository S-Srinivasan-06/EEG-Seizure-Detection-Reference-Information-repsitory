### A minimum evaluation protocol

**Cohort and identity.** Publish patient, session, recording, site, device, and pretraining provenance manifests or a reproducible hashed identity scheme. Split by the unit matching the claim: recording for within-person personalization; patient for person-independent performance; site/hospital for external transportability. Resolve repeated cases (CHB-MIT cases 1/21). Shared hospital data in pretraining is a risk to audit, not proof of leakage. Report supervised and unlabeled target exposure separately.

**Continuous exposure.** Evaluate complete or prespecified continuous records with positive and negative periods preserved. Report usable hours, event counts, seizure-free people, missing channels, artifact burden, interruptions, and event eligibility. Seizure-enriched windows can evaluate discrimination, but cannot supply a natural false-alarm denominator.

**Threshold and postprocessing.** Tune operating points only on development data, lock them before external evaluation, and publish smoothing, minimum duration, event merging, refractory period, and notification rules. Report both event and sample/window measures. A temporal tolerance and short-gap merge change the event score, so state exact values and comparator.

**Reference and uncertainty.** Use independent trained annotators with adjudication and document access to video, clinical context, and competing labels. Report inter-reader agreement and preserve uncertain/ictal-interictal continuum (IIC) patterns. For simultaneous scalp/stereo-electroencephalography (SEEG) work, distinguish (i) detection among independently scalp-visible seizures and (ii) detection among all intracranially confirmed seizures. Avoid classifying all scalp-negative events as model errors without accounting for signal visibility and electrode sampling.

**Performance and statistics.** Report event sensitivity and precision, false alarm episodes per hour/day with denominator, onset delay, offset error, duration/burden coverage, record-level detection, and window/sample metrics as separate endpoints. Provide patient/site macro and pooled estimates with confidence intervals and distributions. Account for repeated events and windows within person; do not treat correlated windows as independent. Stratify age, seizure type, setting, device, and modality where the cohort permits.

**Operational use.** Specify causal vs offline inference, input context, preprocessing, filter phase, buffer/lookahead, compute hardware, failure mode, and full pipeline latency. Measure candidate review time, alarm repetition, staff acknowledgement, appropriate action, and patient outcomes in appropriately designed trials. Model-only metrics do not establish workflow or clinical benefit.

### The SzCORE framework and challenge

SzCORE is a shared framework for harmonized electroencephalography (EEG) data, event annotations, validation scenarios, and scoring. It covers CHB-MIT, TUH/TUSZ, Siena, and SeizeIT1 and describes EEG-BIDS, a standard format for organizing EEG research data, and HED-SCORE, a structured EEG event-labeling scheme. Its Table 1 gives a harmonized inventory: CHB-MIT 23 subjects/982 h/198 seizures/686 files; TUH 675/1,476 h/4,029/7,377; Siena 14/128 h/47/41; SeizeIT1 42/4,211 h/182/458 `[V: SzCORE framework table]`. These are framework summaries, not immutable raw-release manifests. The average-duration value is reported per file (e.g., TUH 10 min/file), not person-hours; accompanying prose conflicts for TUH, so do not derive person exposure from that prose.

The framework proposes resampling to 256 Hz, channel renaming/referencing to common 10-20 channels, EEG-BIDS/HED-SCORE annotations, and three validation scenarios: personalized, subject-independent within-dataset, and subject-independent cross-dataset. CHB-MIT source signals are bipolar and remain in source bipolar montage; missing TUSZ electrodes can be zero-filled in the harmonized representation. These choices aid comparison but do not erase source montage or missing-channel limitations. Personalized evaluation requires adequate per-person data; SzCORE uses at least three seizures and 1.5 h/person, rolling temporal splits with an initial at least 5 h training period and subsequent next-hour tests. It excludes TUSZ from personalization because records are short and most people have fewer than three seizures. [Framework paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18113); [official code](https://github.com/esl-epfl/sz-validation-framework).

SzCORE recommends sample- and event-based sensitivity, precision, F1, and false alarms/day, with per-subject and aggregate reporting. Suggested event scoring includes any overlap as a hit, 30-s pre-onset and 60-s post-offset tolerance, merging gaps under 90 s, and splitting events longer than 5 min into at most 5 min units; these are recommended scoring parameters, not universal clinical definitions. State exact implementation and deviations. Public tools include the [validation framework](https://github.com/esl-epfl/sz-validation-framework), `epilepsy2bids`, `timescoring`, and the [benchmark platform](https://epilepsybenchmarks.com/).

Version the challenge result. SzCORE challenge v2 (arXiv revision 18 May 2026; non-peer-reviewed) evaluates 28 architectures on a strictly held-out private continuous EEG test set of 65 people and 4,360 h; best event F1 is 32%, sensitivity 37%, precision 29% [V]. Superseded v1 reported 43% F1 (37%/45%). The reason for this revision-level metric change was **not found** in the v2 abstract. The private test set prevents independent replication from the public record alone. [v2 record and history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18191v2).

The separate peer-reviewed EMBC review by Samanos, Dan, and Atienza selected 19 eligible papers and reimplemented three [V]. It reports standardized-performance discrepancies; its abstract includes broad >90% sensitivity/10-40% precision figures as well as an approximate 70%/14% conclusion. These are not a coherent pooled clinical estimate and are not reproduced here. A global state-of-the-art (SOTA) adoption count was **not found**. [DOI 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254038](https://doi.org/10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254038); [PMID 41336388](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41336388/).

**High confidence - SzCORE improves comparability by making data and scoring conventions explicit; it does not guarantee a clinical reference standard or bedside utility.** Use it as a protocol and reporting foundation, state departures, preserve modality/site/age strata, and add prospective workflow and outcome evidence for clinical claims.

### What remains to be measured

1. **Consecutive, continuous, seizure-negative exposure** from multiple hospitals with clear site/device identities and release-level manifests.
2. **Locked, external tests** with patient/site disjointness, target-exposure disclosure, and no test-set threshold selection.
3. **Independent, uncertainty-aware reference labels**, including video/context where appropriate and separate uncertain/IIC categories.
4. **Explicit visibility analyses** for scalp vs intracranial detection and realistic sensor dropout/montage change.
5. **Operational outcomes:** candidate review minutes, alarm episodes/duration, staff response, appropriate treatment, safety, and patient outcome, measured prospectively where claimed.
6. **Reproducible full-pipeline latency and compute**, including preprocessing, buffering, artifact handling, missing-channel behavior, and state reset.
7. **Modality-specific evaluation:** no pooled scalp, iEEG, neonatal, ICU, wearable, or multimodal performance estimate absent a justified common target and protocol.
