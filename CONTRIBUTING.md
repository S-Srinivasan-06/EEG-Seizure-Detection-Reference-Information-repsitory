# Contributing

Contributions may correct a factual error, improve an explanation, identify missing primary evidence, or clarify a study's evaluation protocol.

## Proposing a correction

Include the chapter and affected sentence or table row, the proposed correction, and a stable source identifier such as DOI, PMID, PMCID, or an official dataset release. Point to the relevant methods section, table, figure, or supplement. Distinguish the authors' claim from the result their experiment establishes.

If the source is inaccessible, state which material was available. A title, search snippet, or secondary quotation does not establish a detailed methodological claim.

## Adding a study

Record the clinical setting, EEG modality, patients, dataset version, annotation procedure, and training, validation, and test separation. Identify external testing, target-data adaptation, or supervised personalization explicitly.

Keep sample, clip, event, recording, patient, and workflow measures separate. For continuous detection, include monitored exposure, event definitions, false alarms and their denominator, delay, and uncertainty when reported. Write "not found in inspected material" for unresolved fields rather than estimating them.

Label preprints as non-peer-reviewed and separate publication metadata checks from full-text appraisal. New papers do not automatically justify a stronger confidence label.

## Writing conventions

Use clear formal prose, explain abbreviations on first use, and retain evidence labels and confidence reasons. Avoid em dashes, emojis, promotional wording, and unsupported claims of superiority. Use relative links for repository files and stable links for sources. Keep the annotated bibliography at the end of the evidence chapter.

The present edition has an evidence cutoff of 30 September 2026. Later evidence should be identified as an update rather than silently inserted into this historical edition.
